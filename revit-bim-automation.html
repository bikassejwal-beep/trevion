A careful automation process

The aim is to make a repeatable task easier to run and review—not to automate without understanding the model or its risks.

STEP 01

Define the task

Describe the repeated operation, model context, rules, and desired output.

STEP 02

Review the setup

Confirm the Revit version, model standards, parameters, and tool constraints.

STEP 03

Prototype and test

Test on a suitable copy or sample and review the output against expected results.

STEP 04

Document and hand over

Explain how to run the workflow, its assumptions, limits, and review points.

Revit and BIM automation FAQs

What kinds of Revit tasks may be suitable for automation?

Repeated tasks with clear rules—such as selected tagging steps, parameter checks, data extraction, or model-quality checks—may be candidates. Suitability depends on the model, Revit version, workflow, and required safeguards.

Do all BIM tasks work with Dynamo?

No. Dynamo can help with many defined operations, but some needs may require another supported approach or may not be appropriate to automate. The use case should be reviewed before choosing the method.

How do you reduce the risk of unintended model changes?

A suitable workflow should be tested on a copy or sample where possible, compared with expected results, and reviewed by the responsible project team before production use.

What information should I share about a BIM automation request?

Share the Revit version, a short description of the repeated task, the desired result, relevant model standards or parameters, and a safe screenshot or sample if available. Avoid sending confidential project data unless you are authorized to share it.

Have a repeated Revit or BIM task?

Describe the workflow, project setup, and result you need. We can discuss whether automation is practical and what a safe first test could look like.
