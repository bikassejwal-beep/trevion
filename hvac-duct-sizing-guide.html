HVAC Duct Sizing: Airflow, Friction Rate and Pressure Drop

A practical starting point for understanding duct sizing inputs, comparing common sizing methods, and reviewing preliminary results with Trevion's browser-based Duct Sizer Suite.

Open HVAC Tool Read the guide

HVAC duct sizing is the process of selecting duct dimensions that can carry the required airflow while meeting the project's limits for pressure loss, air velocity, noise, space and system performance. A calculator can help compare options, but the design inputs must come from the project requirements.

1. Start with the right design inputs

Before sizing a duct, confirm the airflow for each duct segment, the route length, the available space, and the applicable design criteria. Keep units consistent and check whether the airflow value represents a main trunk, a branch, or a terminal connection.

  • Airflow: use the required design flow for the segment, not a guessed value.
  • Length: enter the straight-duct length when estimating straight-run friction loss. Fittings and equipment losses need separate consideration.
  • Design method: choose a sizing method and criteria suitable for the project and system.
  • Geometry: check the permitted duct width, height, diameter and aspect ratio against the coordinated space.
  • System data: confirm duct material, lining/roughness assumptions, equipment data and relevant manufacturer information.

2. Equal-friction method vs. velocity method

Equal-friction duct sizing

The equal-friction approach selects a duct size using a target friction rate. The calculator estimates a size whose calculated straight-duct friction rate is at or below the entered target, then reports the velocity for review. The target friction rate should be set using the project's design criteria; a sample default is not a universal recommendation.

Velocity-based duct sizing

The velocity method sizes the duct to stay at or below an entered air-velocity limit and reports the resulting friction rate. Choose limits appropriate to the duct section and system requirements. A velocity check alone does not prove that the complete system pressure budget is acceptable.

3. Understand pressure-drop estimates

Pressure loss across a complete HVAC air path may include straight duct friction, fittings, dampers, filters, grilles, coils and other equipment. The Duct Sizer Suite includes several preliminary helpers:

  • Straight duct: estimates friction rate and loss using the entered airflow, size assumptions and straight length.
  • Damper K factor: estimates pressure drop from an entered loss coefficient and face velocity. Obtain the coefficient from applicable product data.
  • Datasheet curve: interpolates between airflow/pressure points entered by the user. Confirm units and stay mindful when estimating outside the supplied range.
  • Filter and plenum: helps compare face area, face velocity and preliminary box dimensions.
  • Louvre and grille: estimates required area and width from airflow, velocity, free-area percentage and a chosen height.

Do not treat a component estimate as a certified manufacturer value. For submittals and final selections, use the applicable manufacturer's published data and project requirements.

4. How to use the Trevion Duct Sizer Suite

  1. Open the HVAC Tool — Duct Sizer Suite.
  2. Under Design basis, choose equal friction or velocity method.
  3. Select the airflow unit (L/s, CFM or m³/h), then enter the project-specific friction rate or maximum velocity as applicable.
  4. Edit the example segment names, airflows and lengths. Example rows are starting data only; replace them with project values.
  5. Review round and rectangular size options, reported velocity, friction rate and estimated straight-duct loss.
  6. Use the sizing/check area to test a constrained duct width or height, then review the status and any warnings.
  7. Use the other tabs for preliminary damper, filter, plenum, louvre and grille checks where relevant.
  8. Record the assumptions and validate the results against the design basis, applicable standards and manufacturer information before using them in a project.

5. What the tool does not replace

The Duct Sizer Suite is a browser-based preliminary helper. It does not automatically verify the complete HVAC system, all fittings and equipment losses, acoustic performance, fire/smoke requirements, balancing, code compliance, or fan selection. Calculations can also be affected by air density, operating conditions, installation details and the accuracy of inputs.

Have a suitably qualified designer review the final ductwork and pressure-loss calculations. Do not use example values or generic coefficients for a construction issue or equipment submittal without verifying them.

Frequently asked questions

What airflow units does the Duct Sizer Suite support?

The duct-sizing and several component calculators support L/s, CFM and m³/h selectors where shown. Check the unit label for each input; some individual calculators use a fixed unit.

Can I use the tool for round and rectangular ducts?

Yes. The duct-sizing area displays round and rectangular options, and includes a check area for comparing a constrained size. Verify the selected size against the actual installation space and project standards.

Does the result include every pressure loss in the HVAC system?

No. Straight-duct loss and the component-specific estimates shown by the tool are not a substitute for a full system pressure-loss calculation that includes all relevant fittings, equipment and operating conditions.

Are the default input values suitable for my project?

Not necessarily. The page displays sample values to demonstrate the calculator. Replace them with approved project values and manufacturer data before relying on the results.
